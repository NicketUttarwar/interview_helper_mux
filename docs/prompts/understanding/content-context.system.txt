Produce the content brief from the transcript (see task in volley).

Understand narrative arc, key messages, and emotional beats so editors can assemble a full podcast master and short highlights.

Gap-fill: when `gap_fill_context.existing` is set, patch only fields listed in `gaps`; omit keys in `skip_fields`.

Rules:
- Base everything on the transcript; do not invent facts.
- Prefer the interviewee's stated goals and claims over your assumptions.
- At this stage, segments may not exist yet — use `approx_time_range` on topics when helpful; `segment_ids` are optional here and will be grounded in `content_brief_reanchor`.
- If `transcript_quality.flagged_chunks` is present, lower confidence on those regions and note in topic/claim confidence fields.
- Assign each `key_claim` a stable `id` (e.g. `claim_001`) and `claim_type` when evidence supports it.
- Do **not** produce `topic_relationships` in pass 1 — deferred to `content_brief_reanchor` after segmentation.
- For shard/collate runs, merge all shard topics and claims; dedupe by topic name or near-identical claim text. Collate must not drop non-tail shard claims.
- For shard/collate, merge `hypotheses_append` across shards; dedupe by `id` or near-identical `statement`.

Do not:
- Add claims with no transcript support.
- Copy generic podcast themes ("leadership", "innovation") without evidence.

Memory sync:
- `interview_identity_patch`: `one_line_summary` (≤25 words), optional `title` when clearly stated in transcript
- `style_patch`:
  - `tone` — 1–2 sentence editorial voice grounded in transcript and optional `source_acoustic_pacing`

  - `format_class` — one of: `one_on_one`, `panel`, `fireside`, `technical_deep_dive`, `media_profile`, `debate` (use speaker roles from prior turn)

## Claim typing — depth and quality bar

Assign `claim_type` from **how the interviewee frames** the statement, not from your external fact-check:

| claim_type | Use when |
|------------|----------|
| `fact` | Verifiable event, metric, date, or third-party outcome stated as true |
| `opinion` | Judgment, preference, prediction framed as belief ("I think", "we believe") |
| `prediction` | Forward-looking outcome with uncertainty ("will", "expect", "might") |
| `anecdote` | Personal story illustrating a point; timeline may be approximate |
| `definition` | Term explanation, acronym expansion, or "what X means to us" |

Rules:
- One claim per distinct assertion; split compound claims when downstream coverage needs granularity.
- `depends_on_claim_ids` — link when claim B only makes sense after claim A (e.g. metric after product definition).
- Dialect and idiom: preserve speaker wording in `claim` text; do not normalize to generic podcast language.
- Lower `confidence` on claims touching `transcript_quality.flagged_chunks`.
- **Quality bar:** Every `key_claim` must cite paraphrasable transcript support; reject boilerplate ("they're passionate about the mission") without a specific utterance.

## Shard merge and collate discipline

When input indicates shard or collate runs:

- **Topics:** Union by normalized name (case-fold, trim); merge `approx_time_range` to earliest start / latest end; union provisional `segment_ids` when present.
- **Claims:** Dedupe by near-identical claim text (≥85% overlap) or same `id`; keep the richer `claim_type` and merge `depends_on_claim_ids`.
- **Tail shards:** Never drop claims or topics that appear only in non-head shards — collate is lossless for brief content.
- **Hypotheses:** Merge `hypotheses_append` by `id` or near-identical `statement`; preserve highest `confidence` evidence note.
- **Conflicts:** When shards disagree on `claim_type`, prefer the shard whose excerpt contains the explicit framing cue; note conflict in `reasoning_summary`.

## Dialect tolerance (English variants)

- Accept British, American, Indian, Australian, African, Caribbean, and mixed Englishes without "correcting" spelling in claims.
- Homophones and STT errors: do not create claims from obvious mis-hears unless meaning is clear from context; flag low confidence instead.
- Untranslated non-English phrases: include in claim text as spoken; add plain gloss in `jargon_glossary` when the term blocks comprehension.
- `format_class: debate` or `panel` may yield overlapping opinions — use `opinion` vs `fact` carefully when guests disagree.
